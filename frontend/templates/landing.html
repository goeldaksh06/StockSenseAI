How it works

From raw price data to an explained forecast

Four steps, all visible in the dashboard — nothing happens in a black box.

1. Pull live market data + compute indicators

OHLCV data from Yahoo Finance, plus RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands, ATR, moving averages, and more — 21 features per ticker, computed fresh on every request.

2. Train and evaluate honestly

Data is split chronologically (train → validation → test, never shuffled) so the model is judged only on data it has never seen — and compared against naive baselines, not just "the actual price."

3. Backtest the trading signal

The model's daily up/down calls are simulated as real trades with transaction costs, and measured against simply buying and holding — return, Sharpe ratio, and max drawdown, side by side.

4. Explain it in plain English

An LLM (Claude / Groq / OpenAI, configurable) reads the indicators, sentiment, and prediction, and writes a short grounded explanation of why the forecast looks the way it does — a separate feature from the prediction itself.

Why this is different

Most stock-prediction demos cheat. This one shows its work.

The single biggest risk in this kind of project is data leakage — letting the model see the future during training, which makes a broken model look great in a demo.

⏳

No shuffling, ever

Time-series data is split purely by date — training only ever happens on the past relative to what's being tested.

📏

Scaler fit on train only

Feature scaling is fit exclusively on training data, then applied to validation/test — never fit on the full dataset.

🎯

Judged against baselines

Every model is compared to previous-close, moving-average, and linear regression — so a "smart" model has to earn its complexity.

A real finding, kept in instead of hidden

Here's what actually happened when this was built:

FAILED

XGBoost trained to predict raw next-day price lost to the naive "tomorrow = today" baseline (RMSE ≈33 vs ≈3). Tree models can't extrapolate past the price range they trained on — and the stock kept climbing past that ceiling during testing.

FIXED

Reframing the target as next-day return and direction (not raw price) solved the extrapolation problem and produced a model with a competitive risk-adjusted backtest (Sharpe ≈1.2–1.4) versus buy-and-hold.

See this live in the dashboard's "Model Performance" table — both the failure and the fix are shown, not just the final number.

FAQ

Common questions

Does this actually beat the stock market? ▾
No — not in raw returns during a strong bull run, and that's stated honestly in the dashboard's backtest results. The best-performing model variant (direction classifier) shows a competitive risk-adjusted return (Sharpe ratio) versus buy-and-hold with a smaller drawdown, but it doesn't out-earn simply holding the stock in this test period. The point of the project is the evaluation rigor, not a claim of beating the market.
What does "directional accuracy" mean? ▾
The percentage of days the model correctly predicted whether the price would go up or down, compared to the previous day's actual price — regardless of whether it got the exact dollar amount right. For a stock, correctly calling the direction is often more useful than nailing the precise price.
Why compare against "previous close" and "moving average"? ▾
Because any model can look impressive next to a raw price chart. The real test is whether it beats doing something almost trivially simple — if a complex XGBoost model can't outperform "assume tomorrow looks like today," that's important to know, not hide.
Is the AI briefing the same as the prediction? ▾
No. The prediction comes entirely from the XGBoost model. The AI briefing is a separate LLM call that reads the model's output, the technical indicators, and news sentiment, and writes a plain-English explanation of the reasoning — it never influences the number itself.
